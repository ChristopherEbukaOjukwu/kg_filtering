What the table shows
Filter   Δ log-units    Cliff δ    Interpretation
F1            1.720       0.636    Survivors have ~5.6× more publications than dropped
F2            2.158       0.780    Survivors have ~8.7× more publications than dropped
F4            2.465       0.835    Survivors have ~11.7× more publications than dropped
All three filters exceed your preregistered threshold (Δ ≥ 0.5 log-units) by 3-5×. All three p-values are computationally 
indistinguishable from zero. Cliff's δ values of 0.6-0.8 are very large effects by conventional standards (anything above 0.474 is "large").
H1 is supported decisively for every filter.

The F4 result is the most interesting finding here
Look at the ordering. The largest attention bias comes from F4 (protein-coding only), not from F1 or F2. 
The filter that's supposed to be a biotype restriction — categorically different from "evidence quality" — actually carries a 
bigger attention gap than either evidence-based filter.
The numbers in plain terms:
Protein-coding survivors have a mean log-pubs of 3.80 (~45 publications per gene).
Non-protein-coding dropped have a mean log-pubs of 1.34 (~3.8 publications per gene).
This is the "curation infrastructure bias" we discussed earlier, now quantified at the cleanest possible level. The biotype filter, 
framed as a categorical structural restriction, is in practice an attention filter — because the same forces that drive what gets 
named "protein-coding" also drive what gets published.

A subtlety worth flagging in your methods
The F4 result has a measurement artifact embedded in it. Recall from earlier: protein-coding genes had 97% gene2pubmed coverage, lncRNAs 
had 20%, pseudogenes ~30%. Some of the F4 attention gap reflects differential curation of publication counts, not differential underlying 
research interest.
