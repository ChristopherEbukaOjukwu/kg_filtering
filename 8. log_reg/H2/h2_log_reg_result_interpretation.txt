# H2 Regression Results

This is the H2 result.

The result is decisive in every direction.

---

## What the Regression Shows

The regression shows three large two-way interactions.

All three are:

```text
positive
large
statistically significant
```

---

## Two-Way Interaction Results

| Interaction | β | exp(β) | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| F1 × F2 | 3.30 | 27.1× | Pairs passing F1 are 27× more likely to also pass F2 than independence predicts |
| F1 × F4 | 1.80 | 6.0× | F1-passers are 6× more likely to be protein-coding than baseline |
| F2 × F4 | 1.55 | 4.7× | F2-passers are 4.7× more likely to be protein-coding than baseline |

---

## H2 Decision Rule

The preregistered H2 criterion was:

```text
|β| ≥ 0.3 for at least 2 pairwise interaction terms
```

The observed result is much stronger than this threshold.

All three pairwise interaction terms have:

```text
β ≥ 1.5
```

This is at least five times larger than the preregistered threshold.

Therefore:

```text
H2 is strongly supported.
```

The effect sizes are not close to ambiguous.

---

# Likelihood-Ratio Test

The likelihood-ratio test comparing the main-effects model to the two-way interaction model gives:

```text
LR = 83,609
df = 3
```

Under independence, this statistic would be expected to be approximately:

```text
3
```

The observed statistic is therefore roughly:

```text
~30,000× larger than expected
```

At this scale, statistical significance is not the main issue. The important point is the effect magnitude, which is enormous.

---

# Three-Way Interaction

The three-way interaction is small but real.

```text
β = -0.73
exp(β) = 0.48
p = 0.001
```

The negative coefficient means that the F1 × F2 super-multiplicative relationship is slightly attenuated when restricted to protein-coding genes.

In simpler terms:

```text
F1 and F2 are strongly correlated, but that correlation is somewhat weaker within protein-coding genes than in the full graph.
```

Mechanistically, this makes sense.

Among non-coding genes, the rare target–disease pair that passes F1 may be more exceptional. It may pass because of a strong curated source rather than broad cross-source aggregation.

(For the chapter, this will be treated as a secondary detail, not a main finding).

# Interpretation for the Chapter

## Main H2 Finding

The F1 × F2 interaction is the headline result.

Stacking F1 and F2 produces:

```text
27× more co-survivors than independence would predict
```

This is not simple redundancy. It is also not independence.

Instead, it is strong positive non-linear compounding.

That is exactly what H2 predicted.

---

## Mechanistic Interpretation

F1 and F2 reward related but non-identical evidence properties.

F1 captures:

```text
strong overall association score
```

F2 captures:

```text
cross-source support
```

They are not measuring the exact same thing.

However, both are partially driven by the same underlying structure:

```text
well-supported gene–disease pairs tend to accumulate both stronger evidence and more evidence sources.
```

As a result, F1 and F2 concentrate together on a smaller population of highly supported target–disease pairs.

In simple terms:

```text
F1 asks whether the association is strong.
F2 asks whether the association is supported by multiple sources.
The pairs that satisfy both are much more common than independence would predict.
```

---

# F1 × F4: Protein-Coding Implicit Selection

The F1 × F4 interaction quantifies implicit protein-coding selection.

```text
β = 1.80
exp(β) = 6.0
```

This means F1-passers are overrepresented among protein-coding genes by a factor of:

```text
6×
```

relative to baseline.

This matches the factorial result:

| Condition | Percent Protein-Coding |
|---|---:|
| Baseline | 97.4% |
| F1 survivors | 99.7% |

The interpretation is:

```text
F1 already implicitly selects for protein-coding targets.
```

Therefore, when F4 is applied after F1, F4 has very little additional work left to do.

# Note on F3

F3 is not included in this regression.

The reason is that the factorial showed F3 has almost no effect on the unfiltered graph.

F3 alone removes only:

```text
1 pair
```

from the unfiltered graph.

Because of this, there is almost no useful variance in the F3 per-pair indicator at this scale.

Including it in the regression would add noise without adding information.

---

## F3 in the Chapter

The key point is:

```text
The unfiltered Open Targets graph is essentially a single connected component.
```

Therefore, the largest connected component filter, which is common in network analysis, has almost no effect on this dataset.

This is not a failure.

It is a structural finding about Open Targets.

It means:

```text
Almost every target–disease pair is already reachable from every other through the aggregated evidence network.
```
