# H3 Results

This is an interesting H3 result, and it is not exactly in the direction originally expected.

The residual lists are especially informative because they show that filtering does not simply separate well-studied genes from poorly studied genes. Instead, the results suggest a more specific pattern:

```text
Filters retain genes with focused, source-diverse disease evidence.
They can remove even highly published genes when their evidence is broad, diffuse, or not concentrated around specific disease claims.
```

---

## Model Fit

| Model | AUC | Δ vs. Previous |
|---|---:|---:|
| M0: `log_pubs` alone | 0.766 | — |
| M1: + constraint | 0.769 | +0.003 |
| M2: + `log_ppi` | 0.770 | +0.001 |
| M3: + chromosome | 0.771 | +0.001 |

---

## Main Model Interpretation

Publication attention does almost all of the predictive work.

Adding structural covariates such as:

```text
constraint
PPI degree
chromosome
```

contributes very little additional predictive power.

The full model has only a small improvement over the attention-only model:

```text
AUC 0.766 → 0.771
```

This suggests that the structural baseline has nearly the same predictive power as publication attention alone.

---

## H3 Criterion

The preregistered H3 criterion required:

```text
AUC < 0.85
```

The observed full-model AUC is:

```text
AUC = 0.771
```

Therefore, the model leaves substantial residual variance unexplained.

This supports H3 because there remains a meaningful gap between:

```text
predicted survival
```

and:

```text
actual survival
```

for many genes.

Examples:

| Gene | Biological Role |
|---|---|

| `NOS3` | endothelial nitric oxide synthase |

| `HLA-DRB1`, `HLA-B` | major histocompatibility complex genes |

Many of these genes have:



The model predicted high survival probabilities for many of them:

```text
predicted survival ≈ 0.87–0.95
```

But they did not survive:

```text
F1 ∧ F2 ∧ F4
```

---

## Why Highly Published Genes May Under-Survive

There are two plausible explanations.

### 1. Broad but Diffuse Disease Evidence

Some highly studied genes may be mentioned across many diseases, but without strong, focused evidence for any single disease association.

For example:

```text
HIF1A may appear in many disease contexts,
but each disease-specific association may be relatively weak.
```

Open Targets association scoring rewards focused evidence anchors.

Broadly studied pleiotropic genes may therefore have many publications but still fail to cross the F1 threshold for specific target–disease pairs.

---

### 2. Concentrated Single-Source Literature Evidence

Some highly studied genes may have evidence concentrated mainly in literature or text-mining sources.

For example:

```text
a cytokine may appear in many cancer, immunology, and metabolism papers,
but much of that evidence may still count as one broad literature-based source.
```

This can make the gene fail F2 if the evidence does not appear across multiple distinct Open Targets data sources.

---

# Core H3 Interpretation

The important result is:

```text
F1 ∧ F2 is not simply filtering for well-studied genes.
```

Instead, it filters for:

```text
focused, source-diverse gene–disease evidence
```

This means highly published genes can still fail if their evidence is:

```text
broad
diffuse
pleiotropic
not concentrated around specific disease claims
not supported across multiple source types
```

A sharper interpretation:

```text
The filters retain genes whose study has crystallized into specific, multiply sourced gene–disease claims.

Genes whose study remains diffuse, even if they are very highly studied, can be filtered out.
```




## Why Low-Attention Genes May Over-Survive

These genes likely survive because they have highly specific curated evidence.

Possible sources include:

```text
ClinGen
OMIM
rare-disease panels
curated Mendelian disease resources
```

A gene with very few publications can survive if it has:

```text
one strong disease-specific curated association
+
one additional corroborating source
```

So the model reveals a threshold effect:

```text
evidence concentration can matter more than evidence volume
```

In simple terms:

```text
A gene with a few focused rare-disease papers can survive,
while a gene with thousands of diffuse biomedical papers may fail.
```

---

# Combined Story

The combined H3 result is:

```text
Filtering is not simply attention selection.
Filtering is evidence-structure selection.
```

The system favors target–disease pairs where evidence is:

```text
focused
source-diverse
curated
disease-specific
```

It can penalize genes whose attention is:

```text
broad
diffuse
pleiotropic
spread across many disease contexts
```

This sharpens the chapter’s central argument.

The issue is not only that filtering amplifies attention.

The more precise issue is:

```text
a structural mismatch between how research attention accumulates
and how filters operationalize evidence quality
```

Research attention often accumulates diffusely around hub genes.

But filters operationalize quality as:

```text
focused multi-source convergence around specific gene–disease claims
```

# Chapter-Ready H3 Result

A possible write-up:

> After controlling for measured research attention, gnomAD constraint, PPI degree, and chromosome, approximately 22% of protein-coding targets under-survive filtering relative to baseline predictions, using residual < -0.3, while approximately 27% over-survive, using residual > +0.3. The under-survivor population is enriched for highly studied pleiotropic genes, including immune cytokines, transcription factors, and master regulators, whose evidence appears broad but lacks focused multi-source disease anchors. The over-survivor population is enriched for low-attention genes with specific curated disease evidence. These results suggest that filters do not simply retain well-studied genes. Instead, they preferentially retain narrowly evidenced target–disease pairs, while broadly relevant biological hubs may be underrepresented in filtered knowledge graphs.

---

## Constraint Interpretation

The relationship between constraint and survival appears to be:

```text
inverted-U shaped
```

Moderately constrained genes, especially bins:

```text
3–5
```

survive most.

The most loss-of-function-intolerant genes, bin:

```text
0
```

are not the most likely to survive after controlling for attention.

They are closer to the baseline.

The most loss-of-function-tolerant genes, bin:

```text
9
```

survive least.

---

## Possible Explanation

The lower survival of the most LOF-intolerant genes may reflect the fact that some highly constrained genes are essential.

Essential genes may be:

```text
embryonic lethal when disrupted
difficult to perturb experimentally
less directly tied to disease-specific therapeutic evidence
harder to study in ordinary disease contexts
```

The lower survival of highly LOF-tolerant genes is more intuitive:

```text
genes that tolerate loss of function may be less likely to have strong disease associations
```

For the chapter, this should be treated as a discussion point rather than a headline result.

# Summary

H3 is supported, but the interpretation is more interesting than the original outline anticipated.

The result is not simply:

```text
high-attention genes survive
```

Instead, the result is:

```text
attention predicts survival, but imperfectly.
Some highly studied genes under-survive because their evidence is broad and diffuse,
while some low-attention genes over-survive because their evidence is focused and curated.
```

The strongest conceptual takeaway is:

```text
Filtered knowledge graphs may privilege evidence concentration over evidence volume.
```
