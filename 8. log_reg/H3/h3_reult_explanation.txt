This is a genuinely interesting H3 result, and not in the direction I was expecting. Let me walk through what the data is saying, because the residual lists are surprising in a useful way.

## The model fit

```
Model                        AUC       Δ vs. previous
M0: log_pubs alone           0.766     —
M1: + constraint             0.769     +0.003
M2: + log_ppi                0.770     +0.001
M3: + chromosome             0.771     +0.001
```

**Attention does ~99% of the predictive work.** Adding constraint, PPI degree, and chromosome each contributes essentially nothing to AUC. Your structural baseline has essentially the same predictive power as attention alone.

Against your preregistration's H3 criterion (AUC < 0.85), this passes: the model leaves substantial residual variance unexplained. AUC of 0.77 means there's a meaningful gap between "predicted survival" and "actual survival" for many genes.

**The model is consistent with H3, but for a different reason than your outline anticipated.** The original framing assumed that structure (constraint, PPI) would carve away some of the attention signal, leaving a structural residual. Instead, attention alone has limited predictive ceiling at 0.77 — and the residuals show patterns that aren't captured by *either* attention or measured structure.

## What the residual lists actually show

**The under-survivors are the most telling result of the entire chapter.**

Look at this list: HIF1A, NOS3, IFNG, ABCB1, MDM2, ADIPOQ, IL4, IL1A, MAPK14, CXCL8, BIRC5, GSTP1, HMGB1, HLA-DRB1, TLR2, IL-10, MKI67, HLA-B...

These are not random under-attended genes. **These are some of the most-studied genes in biomedical research.** HIF1A is the master regulator of hypoxic response. NOS3 is endothelial nitric oxide synthase. IFNG, IL1A, IL4, CXCL8 are major immune cytokines. MDM2 is the canonical p53 regulator. HLA-DRB1 and HLA-B are MHC class II/I — central to the immune system. These genes have log_pubs of 7-8 (1000-3000 publications each) — well within the top 5% of human genes by attention.

The model predicted survival probabilities of 0.87-0.95 for these genes. **They didn't survive F1 ∧ F2 ∧ F4.**

That requires explanation. Two possibilities:

1. **These genes don't have any strong, multi-source disease association.** Despite being intensively studied, the literature about them is broad rather than focused. HIF1A may be mentioned in 1000+ papers about 500 different diseases, each with a weak literature score, none crossing F1's threshold. The harmonic-sum aggregation rewards focused evidence anchors, which broadly-studied pleiotropic genes lack.

2. **These genes' associations are concentrated in single-source literature evidence.** A cytokine mentioned in cancer papers, immunology papers, metabolism papers — many sources by count but each is "literature" so it counts as one source for F2.

Either explanation makes the same point: **F1 ∧ F2 is not filtering for "well-studied" — it's filtering for "studied with focused, source-diverse evidence about specific diseases."** The under-survivors aren't being unfairly excluded; they're being excluded because their evidence pattern is "broad but shallow" rather than "narrow but corroborated."

This is a more sophisticated story than "filters retain well-studied genes." It's: *filters retain genes whose study has crystallized into specific, multiply-sourced gene-disease claims. Genes whose study remains diffuse — even very highly-studied ones — get filtered out.*

**The over-survivors confirm the mirror image.**

OR10R2, OR51V1, NKPD1, RAX2, LRRC75B, MBLAC1... these are mostly olfactory receptors (the OR genes), open reading frames (C1orf141), and other very-low-attention genes. Their `log_pubs` values are 1.6-2.6, meaning fewer than 10 publications each. Yet they survived F1 ∧ F2 ∧ F4.

These genes must have **a single high-quality curated entry** in OT — almost certainly from ClinGen, OMIM, or a rare-disease panel where they're definitively associated with one specific Mendelian condition. One strong source can push them over F1 (high overall score) and across F2 if combined with even one weak corroborating source.

**The combined story:** the system has a *threshold effect* where evidence concentration matters more than evidence volume. A gene with 1 paper showing strong rare-disease evidence beats a gene with 1000 papers showing diffuse pleiotropic mentions.

## What this means for the chapter

H3 is supported with a substantially more interesting story than the original outline:

> *After controlling for measured research attention, gnomAD constraint, PPI degree, and chromosome, ~22% of protein-coding targets under-survive filtering relative to baseline predictions (residual < -0.3), and ~27% over-survive (residual > +0.3). The under-survivor population is enriched for highly-studied pleiotropic genes (immune cytokines, transcription factors, master regulators) whose evidence is broad but lacks focused multi-source disease anchors. The over-survivor population is enriched for low-attention genes with single high-quality Mendelian-disease curation entries. Filters therefore preferentially retain not "well-studied genes" but "narrowly-evidenced gene-disease pairs," with the result that broadly-relevant biological hubs are systematically under-represented in filtered KGs.*

That's a publishable result, and it sharpens the chapter's central argument. The bias isn't simply "attention bias amplified by filtering" — it's "**a structural mismatch between how research attention accumulates (diffusely, around hub genes) and how filters operationalize evidence quality (requiring focused multi-source convergence).**"

## Constraint bin pattern worth noting

A small thing worth flagging: the constraint coefficients are non-monotonic.

```
constraint_bin  coefficient    interpretation
0 (most intolerant, ref)  0    baseline
1                         0.09
2                         0.09
3                         0.25
4                         0.33  ← peak
5                         0.22
6                         0.09
7                        -0.20
8                        -0.27
9 (most tolerant)        -0.50
```

The relationship between constraint and survival is **inverted-U**: moderately-constrained genes (bins 3-5) survive most, with both extremes surviving less. The bin 0 (most LOF-intolerant) genes are *not* the most likely to survive after controlling for attention — they're at the *baseline* level.

This is mildly counterintuitive. It might reflect that the most LOF-intolerant genes are often essential, embryonic-lethal-in-knockout, hard to study in disease contexts because perturbation is incompatible with life. Bin 9 (most tolerant) genes survive least, which is what you'd expect — they're tolerable to lose, less likely to be disease-implicated. The inverted-U is mostly the middle bins (3-5) being elevated.

For the chapter, this is a sentence in the discussion, not a headline. Constraint contributes statistically but in a non-trivial way.
